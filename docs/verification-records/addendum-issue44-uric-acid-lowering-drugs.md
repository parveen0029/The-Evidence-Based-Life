# Issue #44: Adding Uric-Acid-Lowering Drug Choices and Urine Alkalinization to Section 16, Rule 9

2026-09-29. A reader pointed out that Section 16, Rule 9 ("Take uric acid-lowering drugs long-term upon confirmed gout diagnosis") only covered allopurinol, raising four points:

1. Among drugs inhibiting uric acid production, febuxostat is the primary agent, used more widely than allopurinol.
2. Among drugs promoting uric acid excretion (uricosurics), benzbromarone is the primary agent, alongside probenecid and dotinurad; dotinurad may become the future mainstay.
3. When taking benzbromarone to excrete uric acid, alkalinizing urine with sodium bicarbonate is recommended.
4. Basis: Chinese Medical Association "Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Hyperuricemia and Gout in China (2019)" and "Primary Care Guideline for Gout and Asymptomatic Hyperuricemia (2024)".

## Resolution Summary

The core thread of the rule remains unchanged: once gout is diagnosed, adhere to long-term uric acid-lowering therapy and titrate dosages to achieve target serum urate levels. What the reader raised concerns "which drug to choose"; on this level, genuine divergence exists, which the original rule had not unpacked beyond citing the American guideline's first-line choice. Evidence from both sides was added to the end of the Benefit column, and "Controversy" was added to the start of the Remarks.

- Point 1: "More widely used" reflects current prescribing patterns, not evidence. What truly warranted inclusion is the cardiovascular safety debate over febuxostat, now supplemented with both the CARES and FAST randomized trials.
- Point 2: Added a sentence noting "benzbromarone remains available in China." Probenecid and dotinurad were omitted. Information regarding dotinurad's domestic approval was found only in corporate press releases rather than National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) primary texts; "future mainstay" is a speculative forecast and excluded from text.
- Point 3: Here lies a genuine divergence, now detailed in the remarks. The American College of Rheumatology (ACR) guideline conditionally recommends against alkalinizing urine (evidence certainty very low), recommending adequate hydration instead. Concrete recommendation texts from Chinese guidelines could not be retrieved in full, as noted below.
- Point 4: Full texts of domestic guidelines are behind paywalled repositories; only abstracts were retrieved. The reader's second cited source—the Primary Care Guideline (2024)—could not be found even as an abstract; the "2024 updated edition" referenced in the book is an endocrine society guideline ("Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Hyperuricemia and Gout in China (2024)"), which is a distinct document.
