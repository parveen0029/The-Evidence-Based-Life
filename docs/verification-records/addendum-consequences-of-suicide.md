# Addendum: Three Rules on the Realities of Suicide · Verification Record (2026-09-18)

Task Origin: A user asked: "Should we add something about not committing suicide? It feels like the book hasn't detailed how catastrophic the consequences of suicide actually are."

Original Coverage (searching "suicide" across the entire book): Section 1 Rule 25 (restricting lethal means + 12356 helpline), Section 3 Rule 19 (what to do first when feeling depressed), Section 8 Rules 13 and 14 (impulses to harm others, next-of-kin psychiatric referral rights), Section 29 Rule 4 (bereavement risks for suicide-loss survivors), Section 30 Rules 4 and 7 (school bullying, adolescent depression screening), Section 19 (suicide excluded from work-related injury, mentioned in passing in remarks). In other words, "how to seek help" and "what family members should do" were covered, but the exact reality pointed out by the user was missing: what actually happens if someone follows through—the precise time horizon of the suicidal impulse, the long-term prognosis after surviving an attempt, what irreversible damage remains after resuscitation, and the financial reality.

Implementation: Section 1 added Rules 30 and 31 (appended at the end of the section rather than inserted after Rule 25, avoiding renumbering Rules 26 through 29 which would break 6 existing cross-section and verification record citations); Section 29 added Rule 12 (the financial side, appended after "never make irreversible decisions during acute stress"). Section 1 Rule 25 remarks and Section 29 section intro cross-reference guides were updated synchronously.

Adhering strictly to two ethical boundaries: **No comparison of case-fatality rates between different methods, and zero description of specific lethal techniques.** Rule 31 only references two methods already mentioned elsewhere in the book (paraquat from Rule 25, and carbon monoxide from Section 13 Rule 18), focusing entirely on "what physical devastation remains after surviving resuscitation," not "which method is more lethal." The long-term prognosis in Rule 30 is framed as "nine out of ten individuals who survive an attempt do not subsequently die by suicide," while citing Owens's explicit qualification in the remarks that "suicide risk among self-harm patients remains hundreds of times higher than in the general population," ensuring it is never misread as "no treatment is needed."

## Section 1 Rule 30 (Time Horizon of Impulses + Long-Term Outcomes After Attempt)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.07m03904> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Deisenhammer 2009: "Eighty-two patients referred to a psychiatric university hospital after a suicide attempt were approached within 3 days after the act"; "Nearly half of the patients (47.6%; N = 39) reported that the period between the first current thought of suicide and the actual attempt had lasted 10 minutes or less"; "Those patients in which this process had taken longer showed a higher suicidal intent (p < .001). Impulsivity was not associated with the duration of the suicidal process"; "Although the majority of the patients were alone during the suicidal process, 76.8% (N = 63) reported having had any kind of interpersonal contact"; Conclusion: "Professional helpers appear to have limited potential for intervention during this phase. Thus, spreading information on signs of suicidality and interventional measures among the general population should be incorporated into suicide prevention strategies" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.181.3.193> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Owens 2002: "A systematic review of published follow-up data... 90 studies met the inclusion criteria"; "Median proportions for repetition 1 year later were: 16% non-fatal and 2% fatal; after more than 9 years, around 7% of patients had died by suicide"; "Suicide risk among self-harm patients is hundreds of times higher than in the general population" |

Graded B: Deisenhammer involved only 82 patients, single-center, reliant on post-hoc retrospective recall; Owens synthesized median proportions across 90 heterogeneous studies rather than a pooled meta-analytic effect size. The 47.6% statistic was previously cited in Section 8 Rule 13 (for impulses to harm others); here it serves its primary place for self-harm, supplemented by the 76.8% interpersonal contact finding—which directly grounds the action rule (tell one person nearby), and reinforces the author's conclusion that "professional helpers have limited intervention windows during this acute phase, necessitating public education among laypeople."

Benefit magnitude: "large": Dimension is mortality. While there is no relative risk reduction figure from an intervention trial to mechanically apply, per CLAUDE.md guidelines ("use qualitative judgment only when numerical indices cannot be computed"), preventing death itself is evaluated as "large". Costs: Money = 0, Time = low, Willpower = some (reflecting the psychological barrier to speak up in that moment).

## Section 29 Rule 12 (The Financial Dimension)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811> | Yes (API fetched docx, verified verbatim, effective 2015-04-24 revision) | Insurance Law Article 44: "Where the contract designates the death of the insured as the condition for paying insurance benefits, if the insured commits suicide within two years from the date the contract is formed or reinstated, the insurer shall not be liable to pay insurance benefits, except where the insured lacked civil capacity at the time of suicide." "Where the insurer bears no liability to pay insurance benefits pursuant to the preceding paragraph, it shall refund the cash value of the insurance policy in accordance with the contract." |
| <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm> | Retained from Section 19 verified text | Social Insurance Law & Regulation on Work-Related Injury Insurance Article 16, Item (3): "Self-inflicted injuries or suicide" shall not be determined or deemed as work-related injury; Article 39 statutory standards for three work-related death benefits (funeral subsidy equal to 6 months of the prior year's local average monthly wage; pensions for dependent relatives; one-off work-related death subsidy equal to 20 times the prior year's national urban per capita disposable income) |
| <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf> | Yes (Ibid.) | Civil Code Article 1159: "When partitioning the estate, taxes and debts legally payable by the deceased shall be cleared; however, necessary estate shall be preserved for heirs who lack labor capacity and have no source of income." Article 1161: "Heirs shall clear taxes and debts legally payable by the deceased up to the actual value of the estate obtained. The portion exceeding the actual value of the estate is not subject to this limitation if heirs repay voluntarily." "Heirs who renounce inheritance shall bear no liability for clearing taxes and debts legally payable by the deceased." |

Graded A: All three are verbatim verifiable active statutory provisions. Benefit magnitude: "large" mechanically derived under monetary thresholds—life insurance face values and lump-sum work-injury death allowances are both in the hundreds of thousands of yuan.

Two deliberate choices: (1) **Omitted accident insurance**: "Suicide does not constitute accidental injury" is a contract law inference from the "unintentional/external" legal element, but verbatim verifiable regulatory definitions were unretrieved this round (regulatory rules on personal accident insurance are absent from the State Council policy database); thus the text strictly covers life insurance and workers' compensation where statutory text exists. (2) **Placing "Article 44 applies only to the first two years" in the remarks**: Omitting it would mislead readers into believing suicide is perpetually uninsurable; placing it in the main text risks it being read as an incentive timeline, so it is placed in remarks with the explicit caveat that it is included for statutory completeness, not as a schedule.

# Round Two (Same Day): The Medical Bill After Surviving a Fall + Pressure Ulcers from Prolonged Bedrest

Source of Addition: A user shared their personal experience—surviving a fall from a 4th floor, assuming "recovery would take a couple of weeks," but actually enduring a year of hospitalization, four surgical operations, titanium plates, near-fatal emergency resuscitation, six months of total bedridden incontinence and inability to sit up or turn over independently, followed by long-term sequelae and complications; elderly roommates suffered bedsores. Round 1's Rule 31 covered only toxicological ingestion (paraquat, CO); post-fall survival with years-long rehabilitation and complications from prolonged immobility had zero coverage across the book (searching "pressure injury," "bedsore," "repositioning," "bedridden" yielded only a passing mention in Section 17 Rule 7's long-term care insurance).

Implementation: Section 1 added Rule 32 (Surviving a High Fall: ICU, Surgery, Rehabilitation, Inability to Return to Work, and Financial Cost); Section 17 added Rule 8 (Pressure Ulcer Prevention for Prolonged Bedrest, placed after long-term care insurance). Section 17 section opening metric notes, README, and CLAUDE.md directory descriptions were synchronously updated to specify "the pressure ulcer rule is calculated under mortality."

**Lived experience entered remarks only, not evidence**: All figures in the main text derive from verifiable literature. With the user's consent, a brief factual sentence without sensational details was added to the end of Rule 32 remarks, attributed to the author per user request ("The timeline in this rule reflects the author's lived experience: falling from a fourth floor, anticipating 'recovery in two weeks,' but enduring a year of hospitalization, four surgeries, titanium fixation plates, six months unable to turn over independently, and family debt"). It does not affect the evidence grading, nor does it enter the benefits column—all numbers in the body are substantiated independently by literature.

Graded B: The fall cohort is a single-center retrospective study that did not disaggregate accidental falls from intentional self-harm; the return-to-work study evaluated only 84 patients in Germany. **Deliberately avoided correlating height with severity**: The source paper reported only height distributions (median 3 m, 11.4% ≥10 m) without stratified outcomes by height; the text faithfully mirrors this distribution and refrains from calculating "what happens from which floor"—respecting evidence boundaries and upholding the ethical policy against providing data that could inform lethal method selection.

Financial figures provide only a verifiable baseline: 9,870 yuan represents the national mean across all hospitalizations, not major polytrauma admissions, as noted in remarks. The 27.5% out-of-pocket health spending reflects macro national accounting, illustrating that the financial gap falls upon families, rather than serving as an individual copay rate.

Benefit magnitude: "large", costs zero, cost-effectiveness "extremely high"; dimension: mortality (including health endpoints such as ICU admission and prolonged rehabilitation).

## Section 17 Rule 8 (Pressure Ulcers from Prolonged Bedrest)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Porter et al. 2022, 19,520 hip fracture patients (NSQIP 2016–2019): "The presence of a PPU was independently associated with a 21% increase in odds of 30-day mortality (odds ratio (OR) = 1.2, P = 0.004)"; "also independently associated with increased odds of DVT (OR = 1.59, P < 0.001), pneumonia (OR = 1.39, P < 0.001), and unplanned hospital readmission (OR = 1.43, P < 0.001) and a significant increase in the mean length of hospital stay of 0.4 days (P = 0.007)" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Ottaviani et al. 2026, 324 hospitalized elderly patients (mean age 86), median 18-month follow-up: "both frailty (CFS: HR 1.535, 95% CI 1.314-1.793, p < 0.001) and PU (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059-2.126, p = 0.020) were significant predictors of long-term mortality" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract) | Shi et al. 2018, network meta-analysis of 65 randomized trials: "There was moderate-certainty evidence that powered active air surfaces and powered hybrid air surfaces probably reduce pressure ulcer incidence compared with standard hospital surfaces (risk ratios (RR) 0.42, 95% confidence intervals (CI) 0.29 to 0.63; 0.22, 0.07 to 0.66, respectively)" |
| <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4> | Yes (Europe PMC abstract; doi.org redirect to Wiley returned 403 due to publisher bot protection) | Cochrane 2026 Update, 11 trials, 4,462 patients: 2-hour vs 4-hour repositioning "(RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39; 4 trials, 1104 participants), but we judged the certainty of the evidence as very low"; "One trial evaluated real-time wearable patient sensor data with visual reminders for 2-hourly repositioning compared to standard care... reported a significant reduction in pressure injuries with the visual warnings from the sensors (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75). We judged the certainty of this evidence as moderate"; "One trial (116 participants) compared prone versus supine positioning in ICU patients, and found there may be higher pressure injury incidence in the prone group (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98)" |

Graded B: The mortality associations are observational correlations (patients with pressure ulcers are inherently frailer and more severely ill), and cannot be interpreted causally as "preventing pressure ulcers directly lowers mortality"; randomized trial evidence for rigid repositioning intervals has very low certainty. The rule therefore frames the action as "what matters is whether turning is actually carried out, rather than obsessing over rigid clocks"—the moderate-certainty finding was specifically "using sensor visual alerts to ensure turning is actually executed."

Alternating pressure mattresses: The source literature compared them against standard hospital surfaces; remarks note that home use is an extrapolation. Anatomical inspection sites and "non-blanchable erythema" represent standard nursing clinical guidelines, detailed in the text with remarks noting lack of standalone individual citations.

Costs: Money = low (home electric alternating pressure air mattresses start from several hundred yuan), Time = high (repositioning every 2–3 hours, including through the night), Willpower = Yes; Benefit = large; cost-effectiveness rated "average"—demanding substantial effort, this intervention is far from effortless.
