# Addendum: Food Safety (Buyer Claims, Seller Licensing, Labeling, and Therapeutic Red Lines) · Verification Record (2026-09-20)

Task Origin: The author asked whether the book contained food safety content, citing four questions: Can buyers claim tenfold punitive damages with a 1,000-yuan minimum when purchasing expired food or food with hair in it? Can food sellers advertise disease-treating therapeutic effects? What licenses do ordinary citizens need to sell pork or homemade food? Do packaged foods require printed production dates?

Original Coverage (Audit findings): Searching the book for terms like "Food Safety Law, tenfold, production date, shelf life, food production license, small workshop, bulk food, food stalls, homemade, slaughter, pork, quarantine" yielded zero hits, save for isolated irrelevant occurrences of "shelf life," "prepackaged," and "quarantine." Only three tangential references existed: Section 5 Rule 29 (online shopping rules) mentioned Consumer Rights Protection Law threefold damages for fraud (minimum 500 yuan); Section 6 Rule 10 (avoiding expensive health supplements) noted health food packaging must state "cannot substitute pharmaceutical drugs"; Section 12 Rule 7 (licensed trades) mentioned in the cost column that "catering and food sales require food business permits, while selling solely prepackaged foods requires only filing." Buyer tenfold compensation, seller four-tier licensing, mandatory labeling items, and therapeutic advertising bans had zero landing points across the book.

Implementation: Added 4 new rules. Section 5 added Rule 31 (buyer claims), inserted after Rule 30 (problems with goods purchased in livestreaming rooms), shifting original Rules 31 through 34 to Rules 32 through 35; Section 12 added Rules 8, 9, and 10 (licensing / labeling / therapeutic claims), inserted after Rule 7 (licensed trades), shifting original Rules 8 through 19 to Rules 11 through 22. Section 12 Rule 7 remarks added a cross-reference pointing to the new Rule 8. Pork sales and quarantine certificates are incorporated into the new Rule 8 (for readers, this represents checking suppliers' accompanying quarantine certificates rather than obtaining a personal butcher license); bulk food labeling was integrated into the new Rule 9. Following insertion, `node tools/check-refs.mjs --check` passed (321 references verified with valid anchors), and `docs/reference-comparison.md` diff confirmed all shifted rule numbers pointed to unchanged headings.

Two Key Rebuttals (Correcting Author Assumptions):
1. "Finding a stray hair in food entitles a buyer to tenfold compensation with a 1,000-yuan minimum" is legally untenable. The statutory threshold for tenfold compensation requires food to fail food safety standards; whether a foreign object meets this statutory threshold typically requires administrative confirmation or laboratory appraisal. Furthermore, the statute explicitly exempts label and packaging defects that do not impair safety or mislead consumers, reinforced by Fa Shi [2024] No. 9 Article 8's enumerated non-actionable defect list.
2. Tenfold compensation is not unlimited: For professional plaintiffs or repeated buyers who "knowingly purchase substandard food to litigate repeatedly," courts uphold claims strictly "within the scope of reasonable living consumption needs." Ordinary consumers eating normally compute tenfold based on actual purchase amounts paid, secured by Article 1 Paragraph 2 of the Interpretation. Both caveats are reflected in the plain-language text and remarks.
