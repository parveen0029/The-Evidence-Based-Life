# Addendum: Justifiable Self-Defense · Verification Record (2026-09-20)

Task Origin: The author inquired whether the book currently covered justifiable self-defense. Verification confirmed "mentioned in passing, but no dedicated rule," and the author approved adding a new rule.

Original Coverage (Audit findings): Searching the entire book for "justifiable self-defense / excessive defense / special self-defense / unlimited self-defense" yielded only three hits, all of which were isolated sentences within other rules: Section 8 Rule 9 (report conflicts first without fighting back) noted that mutual combat is not self-defense and stated the two elements of excessive defense; Section 13 Rule 37 (extorted by strangers) used the temporal boundary that "once the extortionist takes the money and turns to leave, you cannot pursue"; Section 13 Rule 38 (encountering a brawl) applied the principle that "defense also extends to protecting others." All three urged retreat with the consistent guidance: do not fight back. The genuine gap was the other half: when you cannot retreat, to what extent can you fight back without facing criminal countersuits? Article 20, Paragraph 3 of the Criminal Law on special self-defense had zero hits across the book, and the procedural costs prior to self-defense recognition (case filing, detention) similarly had zero hits.

Placement: Added Rule 10 to Section 8, inserted after Rule 9 (report conflicts first without fighting back), shifting original Rules 10 to 38 to Rules 11 to 39. Placed in Section 8 rather than Section 13 because this rule addresses legal boundaries and post-incident procedural costs rather than immediate seconds-level emergency tactics on scene. Following the rule shift, repo-wide references were synchronized across two passes: 65 instances in rule fields across book/ and docs/ (evaluated by scripts handling "this section / Section 8 / bare rule numbers"), and another 29 instances of "Section 8 Rule N" in docs/ long-form articles and verification records. `node tools/check-refs.mjs --check` passed (all 313 references correctly pointed with anchors), and line-by-line diff review of `docs/citation-cross-reference.md` confirmed every line shifted the rule number while preserving target titles without misalignment.

## Omitted from Main Text

- Defensive devices (setting electric fences, animal traps, etc.) omitted: Not covered by the Guiding Opinions, marked by substantial judicial disagreement, and lacking verbatim quotable official standards.
- The term "unlimited right of self-defense" avoided: Both statutes and Guiding Opinions term it "special self-defense"; the former is pop-legal terminology.
- Specific sentencing ranges for convicted excessive defense omitted: Only individual case outcomes exist (Guiding Case No. 46, Zhu Fengshan case, resentenced to 7 years on appeal) without official statistical distributions; citing a single number risks being treated as a universal benchmark.
- Unlawful entry into residences not broken out as a standalone tier: Guiding Opinions Article 5 states "defensive acts may be undertaken against unlawful entry into residences...", which is subsumed under the general "unlawful infringement" wording; singling it out would crowd out the three core boundary conditions.
- Daily state compensation amounts are not duplicated here, redirecting to Section 8 Rule 34 (state compensation following wrongful detention).
