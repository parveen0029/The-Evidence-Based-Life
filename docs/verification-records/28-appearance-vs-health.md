## Regarding the treatment of the "MTF" half sentence (writing decision, not fact checking)

The user's original words include "Follow the trend of online MTF, and the gender is not defined." This section is **not** written as "Don't follow the transgender trend", but is rewritten as Article 7: "Sex hormone drugs should only be used under the premise of being prescribed by a doctor and reviewed regularly. Do not buy them online and do not increase the dosage yourself." There are three reasons:

1. The evidence is consistent with the method of drug use, not the motivation for drug use. Getahun 2018 measured the difference in vascular events between transgender women and cisgender controls. It supported the conclusion that "people taking estrogen need long-term vascular monitoring" but did not support the conclusion that "it should not be used." To write it as "discourage" is to use this data to say something it did not say, which violates the rule of this project that "the numbers must be found in the original text."
2. Standardized medical pathways exist in China (endocrinology or corresponding specialist assessment, prescription, and follow-up). If we try to dissuade them along with the black market channels, the actual effect is to push people towards the latter - which is the path with the highest risk.
3. The same rule naturally covers the remaining two items listed by the user: self-dosing of birth control pills/menopausal hormones/testosterone supplements, and anabolic steroids in No. 5. Written as "prescription + regular review", it is more accurate and shorter than classifying by group of people.

The introduction of this section states that "it does not evaluate what anyone wants to become" and limits the scope to the risk difference between "having a prescription, having qualifications, and having a review" and "online shopping, informal institutions, and self-increasing dosage". "Feeling ugly" mentioned by users has been moved to item 8 (body dysmorphic disorder should be evaluated first), which is the only point in this part of the appeal that has quantitative literature support.

## Out of Scope / Not Performed

- The association of social media use with body dissatisfaction was not written. Most of the meta-analyses in this direction are cross-sectional correlations (r values), with small effect sizes and unclear causal directions. They can only be rated C when written in, which does not match the evidence strength of the other items in this section. The user's comment "Don't follow the trend" was changed to point out "Decisions mostly come from comparing with others" in the introduction of the section instead of separate items.
- The indications and abuse of GLP-1 weight loss drugs (semaglutide, etc.) are not written. It changes rapidly, domestic indications and medical insurance coverage are being adjusted, and the "prescription + doctor's judgment" in Article 6 of this section has already covered behavioral recommendations; if you want to list it separately, you need to check the current instructions and indication approval documents separately, and leave them for follow-up.
- There is no official typical case of illegal beauty. CLAUDE.md only requires cases for Section 9, this section is not required; and the Ministry of Public Security site is on this machine at 521, so first-hand reports are not available.
