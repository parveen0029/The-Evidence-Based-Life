# Addendum: Two Rules on Erectile Dysfunction + One Rule on Prostitution · Verification Record (2026-09-21)

Task Origin: GitHub issue #21, titled "'Sexual repression'—does it count as a health problem in this era?", body empty, no comments.

**The original question was not drafted directly as a rule.** Whether something "counts as a health issue" is a conceptual value judgment, lacking quantifiable endpoints and actionable reader behavior. Under the book's methodological rules, such a proposition could only land as a Grade C opinion piece, which we decline to write. Instead, we extracted three actionable, quantifiable issues from this topic domain.

Original Coverage: Searching across the book for terms like "sex," "erection," "prostitution," "solicitation," and "sexually transmitted diseases," sexual health was touched only in Section 1 Rules 28 and 29 (condoms, HIV testing; renumbered after this round to Rules 30 and 31) and Section 13 Rule 38 (72-hour PEP); erectile function appeared only once as an adverse side effect in Section 28 Rule 3 (anabolic steroids); prostitution appeared only in passing when citing entertainment venue regulations in Section 22 Rule 4 ("prominent warning signs prohibiting prostitution"). As standalone rules: zero coverage.

Implementation:
- Section 1 added Rules 28 and 29, inserted after Rule 27 (gross hematuria). Placed here because Rule 28 shares the exact clinical profile of Rule 27—a painless, non-acute bodily signal where an evaluation can detect life-threatening pathology years ahead. **The insertion shifted original Rules 28 through 34 to Rules 30 through 36**; cross-section citations were synchronously updated across 8 places (Section 6 Rule 18, Section 9 Rule 22 in two places, Section 13 Rule 38, Section 17 Rule 8, Section 29 section intro and Rule 13), and intra-section citations updated across 3 lines / 5 places. The diff of `docs/reference-comparison.md` was verified line by line: all changed lines represented updated rule numbers pointing to unchanged targets, with zero orphaned references. `node tools/check-refs.mjs --check` passed, with total reference count increasing from 504 → 514 (10 new citations, matching new additions).
- Section 9 added Rule 23 (prostitution / soliciting commercial sex), appended to the end of the section without shifting existing rules.

Deliberately Omitted:
- **Coital frequency and mortality** (e.g. Caerphilly cohort): A single observational cohort with profound reverse causality (healthier individuals have more sexual activity); including it would saddle the rule with intractable "Controversy" tags where noise exceeds value.
- **Dogmatic assertions that "sexual repression is harmful" or "harmless"**: No quantifiable, verbatim verifiable evidence was retrieved in this round.
- **Commercial "vigor-enhancing" health supplements adulterated with sildenafil** were not made into a standalone rule: Inappropriate for Section 28 (which focuses on physical appearance); instead compressed into a one-sentence note in Rule 28 remarks, citing the FDA/DailyMed package insert contraindication verbatim.

## Metrics and Magnitude Determination

- Section 1 Rule 28 (Investigating Cardiovascular Health Upon Erectile Dysfunction): Metric: mortality; benefit magnitude recorded as "medium". The 44% figure represents a risk differential between two cohorts, not an intervention trial effect size showing "how much mortality drops from a single clinical visit"; thus it does not mechanically trigger "large" (≥20%). However, unlike gross hematuria (Rule 27) which provides only a diagnostic yield signal, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia identified here have established, highly effective therapies; thus it is not recorded as "small". Rationale documented in remarks.
- Section 1 Rule 29 (Lifestyle Modification): Metric: mortality; benefit magnitude recorded as "small". The clinical endpoint is limited to the IIEF-5 questionnaire score, which is a surrogate endpoint; per thresholds, surrogate endpoints map to "small". Hard endpoints are addressed in Section 2 Rules 1 and 33 and Section 1 Rule 7, cross-referenced in remarks.
- Section 9 Rule 23 (Commercial Sex / Prostitution): Metric: freedom; benefit magnitude recorded as "medium". Avoiding administrative detention corresponds to "medium" under freedom criteria. The crime of transmitting STDs reaches "large" (criminal incarceration), but requires the specific legal element of "knowing one suffers from a serious STD," which is not the baseline default scenario; thus rated under default administrative standards.
