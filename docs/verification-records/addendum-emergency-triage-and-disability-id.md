# Addendum: Emergency Room Triage, Foreign Bodies and Fractures, Disability Assessment and Disability Cards

2026-09-10. Prompted by three questions raised by readers: How hospital medical workflows actually operate (e.g. news reports of individuals queuing at registration counters with knives stuck in their heads); what immediate first aid steps to take for severe trauma and fractures on scene; and whether one should apply for an official Disability Certificate. Auditing revealed no existing rules covering these three areas; assigned according to subject matter to Section 13 (Field First Aid) and Section 24 (Medical Care Procedures and Post-Treatment Formalities), without creating new sections.

Section 13 added Rules 41 and 42; Section 24 added Rules 8, 9, 10, and 11.

## Section 24 Rule 8 (Emergency Pre-examination and Triage)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6951260.htm> (General Office of the National Health Commission, "Notice on Promulgating Healthcare Quality Control Indicators for 6 Specialties Including Emergency Medicine (2024 Edition)", Guo Wei Ban Yi Zheng Han [2024] No. 150, April 30, 2024) | Yes | Annex 1 Indicator 4 "Emergency Graded Triage Execution Rate (EM-ME-04)" description: "Emergency pre-examination and triage must implement precise grading based on scientific criteria; currently widely used standards classify patients into four tiers by acuity: Level I critical/perishing patients, requiring immediate resuscitation; Level II severe/emergent patients, where evaluation and resuscitation frequently occur concurrently; Level III urgent patients, requiring diagnosis and treatment within a short duration; Level IV non-urgent or sub-urgent patients, seen sequentially in standard clinical areas." |
| <http://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/ylws/201912/t20191216_1242338.html> (Beijing Municipal Health Commission, "Work Plan for Strengthening Hospital Emergency Pre-examination and Triage Grading in Beijing" and Annex "Hospital Emergency Pre-examination Triage Grading Standards in Beijing (Trial)", Dec 2019) | Yes | "Adhere to the principle of prioritizing critically and severely ill patients. Reasonably sequence clinical queues from severe to mild, and from volatile instability to relative stability." "Establish emergency pre-examination and triage areas in prominent locations at emergency department entrances." "Follow the 'three zones and four tiers' framework... categorizing into Perishing, Critical, Urgent, and Non-urgent." Adult Table: Level 1 Perishing (Red): "Ongoing or imminent life threat or rapid deterioration, requiring immediate aggressive intervention; Disposition: Immediate evaluation and resuscitation, direct admission to resuscitation room"; Level 2 Critical (Red): "Immediate vital signs monitoring, treated within 10 minutes, direct admission to resuscitation/shock room"; Level 3 Urgent (Yellow): "Prioritized ahead of Level 4, wait in standard waiting area; if wait time exceeds 30 minutes, re-evaluate"; Level 4 Non-urgent (Green): "Queued sequentially, long wait times unless condition shifts; re-evaluate if wait exceeds 4 hours." Level 1 objective vital thresholds include: HR >180 bpm or <40 bpm, SBP <70 mmHg, SpO2 <80% with tachypnea, shock, acute confirmed myocardial infarction, acute altered consciousness/unresponsive or responding only to pain (GCS <9). |

Graded A: Definitions for the four-tier system originate from operative NHC national policy documents; response timeframes originate from Beijing municipal enforcement standards; both are verbatim verifiable. Metric: "time"—this rule recovers avoided clinical deterioration caused by being trapped in administrative queues, rather than a quantifiable standalone mortality drop. Benefit magnitude: "medium": In the absence of a relative reduction percentage from an RCT, qualitative assessment rates it medium (moving a critical patient into a resuscitation room within 10 minutes is a substantial clinical difference, but the rule guarantees queue priority rather than clinical outcome). Costs: Money = 0, Time = low, Willpower = No, synthesizing to "high" cost-effectiveness.

Unretrieved: Ministry of Health's Guidelines for Emergency Department Construction and Management (Trial) (2009) is the historical root of "three zones and four tiers," but nhc.gov.cn returned 412; health industry standard WS/T 390-2012 was a scanned image PDF without a text layer. Thus, neither was cited in sources, citing instead the verifiable 2024 NHC quality indicators and Beijing implementation scheme.

Graded A: Standard text is verbatim verifiable. Metric: "money", benefit magnitude: "large"—the grade directly governs disability damages compensation (statutory calculation multiplying prior year urban per capita disposable income by 20 years per Section 9), easily reaching 10,000+ RMB magnitude. Costs: Money = low, Time = medium, Willpower = some; cost score 3, cost-effectiveness "average".

Appraisal fees of "several hundred to two thousand yuan" are noted in the cost column as common regional forensic appraisal fee ranges; since no single uniform national price decree exists, they are not framed as numerical claims in the benefit column, noting that the applicant prepays the fee.

Metric: "money", benefit magnitude: "medium"—the card itself does not disburse funds; the two connected national subsidies (living subsidy and nursing care subsidy verified in Section 7 Rule 9) disburse dozens to hundreds of yuan monthly, falling short of the 10,000+ RMB threshold. Costs: Money = low, Time = medium, Willpower = some; cost score 3, synthesizing to "average" cost-effectiveness.

## Section 13 Rules 41 and 42 (Penetrating Foreign Objects, Fractures)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf> (IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025, official PDF) | Yes | Pages 256–257: "First aid providers should apply pressure around an embedded object (e.g. a knife) to stabilize the object. Avoid removing the object." Pages 267–268: "Impaled objects in the body should be stabilized, and if the object is pulsating, it should be allowed to do so while loosely stabilized." "First aid providers should not push internal organs back into the body." "Do not remove any impaled objects from an abdominal or chest wound, as this may cause serious bleeding and make it more difficult for a medical professional to determine the severity of the wound." "An open chest wound that is not actively bleeding may be left open, without the application of a dressing, because a sealed wound may allow air to build up in the chest, which may lead to the lung partially or fully collapsing (pneumothorax)." "First aid providers should access emergency medical care for all penetrating chest or abdominal wounds." Pages 301–302: "Any injury to an extremity should be approached as a potential bone fracture." "The injury should be stabilized to minimize any movement and pain either manually (e.g. self-splinting) or mechanically (e.g. using a sling and bandages)." "first aid providers may ice or cool the sprained joint or soft tissue injury for up to 20 consecutive minutes, as this may decrease the pain. Cooling for longer than this may damage the skin." "If a long bone is injured, such as the femur, the bleeding from the bone itself can be life-threatening." "The only accurate way to diagnose a fracture is with an X-ray. When in doubt, assume it is a fracture." "Access emergency medical services (EMS) if the person has a lot of pain or swelling, signs of shock, the fracture is in the femur or if the limb is in an abnormal position." "When in a remote environment (or one with limited resources) and the limb below the angulated fracture is cool, pale or the first aid provider is unable to splint for movement, the first aid provider may consider straightening it if trained to do so." "If the injury is an open fracture, your priority is to stop the bleeding and limit infection." Evidence subsection: Sole retrospective cohort on pre-hospital traction splinting for femoral fractures demonstrated "a statistically significant decrease in units of blood transfusion needed within the first 24 hours and a statistically significant shorter length of hospital stay", but "a statistically significant decrease in mortality and complications could not be demonstrated, and the evidence was of very low certainty"; on reduction vs in-situ splinting: "no evidence was found". |

Both graded B: Guidelines classify these recommendations as good practice points without randomized trials; traction splinting rests solely on a single very-low-certainty retrospective cohort, and reduction versus in-situ immobilization possesses zero comparative evidence. Rule 41 benefit magnitude: "large"—mortality dimension, guarding against immediate fatal exsanguination and tension pneumothorax caused by dislodging impaled objects; Costs: Money = 0, Time = low, Willpower = some; cost score 1, cost-effectiveness "high". Rule 42 benefit magnitude: "medium"—primarily guards against secondary nerve/vascular injury and chronic deformity rather than immediate death (severe femoral hemorrhage being the exception, but the core action is immobilization and icing); costs zero, cost-effectiveness "high".

Unretrieved / Omitted: NICE NG37 (complex fractures) returned 403; 2020 ILCOR international consensus (doi:10.1161/cir.0000000000000897) did not cover impaled penetrating foreign objects; Red Cross Society of China training manuals lack publicly verifiable digital text.
