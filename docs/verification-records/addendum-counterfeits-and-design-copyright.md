# Addendum: Sourcing Counterfeits and Graphic Copyright · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task Origin: A reader raised two real-world scenarios: "Many merchants sell goods where employees down the line sourced counterfeits and were criminally sentenced—specifically selling cosmetics"; and "Clothing sellers printing copyrighted images owned by others were arrested too," adding their own view: "They didn't alter it at all; I feel one should at least modify it a bit, add an icon, change a color, etc."

Original Coverage: Copyright in Section 11 only covered software, open-source licenses, and work made for hire (Rules 11 and 12), directed at employed technical personnel; Section 12 Rule 19 remarks had only a brief cautionary note: "Before manufacturing, check the CNIPA trademark database for similar prior registrations before applying; statutory provisions of the Trademark Law are not audited in this section, offered merely as a reminder." The two critical red lines for entrepreneurs—"whether sourced merchandise is counterfeit" and "whether graphics used belong to others"—were entirely absent across the book. Implementation: Section 12 added 2 new rules (Rules 16 and 17); original Rules 16 and 17 shifted to Rules 18 and 19; intra-section reference "see Rule 17 of this section" (exit rule) updated to Rule 19; the reminder in Rule 15 remarks was updated to point to the new rule. No external sections cite Section 12 by rule number.

**The reader's misconception must be explicitly corrected in the main text**: Altering colors or adding icons does not constitute a legal defense. Grounded in two statutory provisions: Article 52, Item (6) of the Copyright Law lists "using works by means of adaptation, translation, annotation, etc." alongside direct reproduction as infringing conduct; the Trademark Law explicitly includes "using a trademark similar to a registered trademark on identical goods, or using a trademark identical or similar to a registered trademark on similar goods, likely to cause confusion" directly within actionable infringement—"similarity" falls directly within legal crosshairs. Therefore, the title of Rule 17 is explicitly framed: "Changing a Color or Adding an Icon Does Not Count as 'Sufficient Modification'."
