## Section 13 Rule 4 (Amaurosis Fugax and Central Retinal Artery Occlusion)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> (PMID 33677974) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | "Acute CRAO is a medical emergency. Systems of care should evolve to prioritize early recognition and triage of CRAO to emergency medical attention." Duration of ischemia is inversely proportional to viable retinal tissue. |
| Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study). Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2> (PMID 17928046) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | "the 90-day risk of recurrent stroke in the patients referred to the study clinic was 10.3% (32/310 patients) in phase 1 and 2.1% (6/281 patients) in phase 2" |

Rated B rather than A: EXPRESS is a sequential before-and-after comparison in the same population rather than a randomized trial; the effect size includes secular trend components, as noted in the remarks. Benefit magnitude "Large" (mortality/disability metric), cost is 0 yuan plus half a day, cost-performance tier is High.

## Section 13 Rule 9 (Deep Vein Thrombosis and Pulmonary Embolism)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Goldhaber SZ, Visani L, De Rosa M (1999). Acute pulmonary embolism: clinical outcomes in the International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER). Lancet, 353(9162), 1386-1389. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(98)07534-5> (PMID 10227218) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | 2,454 consecutive acute pulmonary embolism patients across 52 hospitals in 7 countries; 3-month all-cause mortality 17.4% (426/2,454), 15.3% after excluding 61 cases diagnosed only at autopsy |
| WHO (2007). Study results released on travel and blood clots. <https://www.who.int/news/item/29-06-2007-study-results-released-on-travel-and-blood-clots> | Yes (Original page verbatim) | "the risk of developing venous thromboembolism (VTE) approximately doubles after travel lasting four hours or more"; "the absolute risk of developing VTE, if seated and immobile for more than four hours, remains relatively low at about 1 in 6000" |

Rated B: ICOPER is observational data from a registry, and WRIGHT is an official agency report; neither provides an effect size on "early recognition altering clinical outcomes." Benefit magnitude "Large." The remarks explicitly note that 17.4% is the mortality rate among diagnosed cases, not the risk among individuals with swollen legs, preventing reader misinterpretation.

Omitted / Not cited reference: Barritt DW, Jordan SC (1960). Anticoagulant drugs in the treatment of pulmonary embolism. A controlled trial. Lancet, 1(7138), 1309-1312 (DOI 10.1016/s0140-6736(60)92299-6). This is the classic controlled trial of anticoagulation for PE. DOI and provenance were verified, but Europe PMC has no abstract and verbatim fatality counts could not be extracted in this round, so its numbers are not cited.

## Section 1 Rule 27 (Painless Gross Hematuria)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Price SJ, Shephard EA, Stapley SA, Barraclough K, Hamilton WT (2014). Non-visible versus visible haematuria and bladder cancer risk: a study of electronic records in primary care. British Journal of General Practice, 64(626), e584-e589. <https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp14X681409> (PMID 25179073) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | 4,915 bladder cancer cases (≥40 years) vs. 21,718 controls; positive predictive value of visible hematuria for age ≥60: "2.8% (95% CI = 2.5 to 3.1)", age 40–59: "1.2% (95% CI = 0.6 to 2.3)"; non-visible hematuria for age ≥60: 1.6% (1.2–2.1), age 40–59: 0.8% (0.1–5.6), odds ratio "20 (95% CI = 12 to 33)" |

Rated B: Single case-control study conducted in a UK primary care population; detection rates in China may differ. Benefit magnitude is mechanically classified as "Small" under the project's rubric — it only provides a surrogate endpoint (detection rate) without relative mortality reduction, while cost is several hundred yuan, placing cost-performance in "Moderate." This rationale is explicitly documented in the remarks so readers do not misinterpret a "Small" rating as an indication that testing is unnecessary.

Placed in Section 1 rather than Section 13: This is not an emergency requiring "what to do in the immediate minutes," but an alarm sign where "delays should be avoided." It belongs to early detection alongside Section 1 screening rules; hence, it was appended to the end of Section 1 and the scope description in the section introduction was adjusted accordingly.
