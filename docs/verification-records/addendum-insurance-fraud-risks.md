# Addendum: Two Rules on Insurance Fraud Risks · Verification Record (2026-09-18)

Task Origin: A reader asked: "Are there rules covering insurance fraud? I see many people online harboring the illusion that they won't get caught—taking out life insurance policies on family members and then brutally murdering them to claim payouts, only to be uncovered anyway. If not covered, please add."

Original Coverage: Searching the entire book for "defrauding insurance," "insurance fraud," and "insurance scam" yielded only one sentence in the remarks of Section 5, Rule 13 (health insurance family mutual-aid account): "Taking it to pharmacies to buy healthcare supplements for cash conversion constitutes insurance fraud." Crimes of insurance fraud, murdering for insurance payouts, and faking accidents for claims had zero coverage.

Placement: Added Rule 36 to Section 8 (the tier involving human lives: four legal barriers + concurrent punishment for multiple crimes + official case study) and Rule 21 to Section 9 (the tier everyday individuals are more likely to encounter: faking accidents, exaggerating losses, arranging false witnesses, and how anti-fraud investigations work). Both rules cross-reference each other, and the introductions in README and CLAUDE.md for Sections 8 and 9 were synchronized.

**Source Replacement Note for Criminal Law**: The Section 9 intro originally stated that Criminal Law citations were taken from the Beijing Traffic Management Bureau reprint. ## Case Study (Zhang Mousong Case in Xuancheng, Anhui)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <http://www.ah.jcy.gov.cn/jczt/jjslhyqkjc/mtjj/202001/t20200113_2758841.shtml> | Yes (Anhui Provincial People's Procuratorate website reprinting Legal Daily 2020-01-13, full text retrieved word for word; UTF-8 encoded) | "Zhang Mousong was sentenced to death with a two-year reprieve and fined 50,000 yuan for the concurrent crimes of intentional homicide and insurance fraud; Wang Mouxin and 8 others were sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment ranging from two to four years for insurance fraud, each fined 10,000 yuan"; "Around 3 a.m. on July 18, 2016, inside a gas station on Xuanjiu Section, Feicai Office, Xuanzhou District, Xuancheng City"; "After conducting on-site investigations, police found no braking marks on the vehicle prior to entering the water, and the tire tracks crushing the roadside turf were completely straight, determining that the vehicle rushed straight into the water without braking maneuvers, inconsistent with normal evasive operations"; "Less than half a month prior to the incident, Zhang Mousong purchased 4 insurance policies for Guo Mou, with each insured amount being 1 million yuan. Two days before the incident, Zhang searched online repeatedly for 'car sinking in water video', 'how to self-rescue from car in water', 'how long until drowning causes death', and similar queries"; "On the day of the incident, Zhang phoned the insurance company to report the claim, and on the very next day searched online for 'statutory beneficiary distribution ratio'"; "On July 25 that year, the Kaifaqu Branch of Xuancheng Municipal Public Security Bureau arrested Zhang Mousong. Confronted with evidence, Zhang confessed that he orchestrated a traffic accident to murder his wife and claim insurance payouts to settle debts"; "The Material Evidence Appraisal Center of Xuancheng Public Security Bureau identified that Guo Mou died of drowning"; regarding the false litigation aspect: "Afterward the insurance company assessed damage at 20,000 yuan... the court awarded over 70,000 yuan in compensation," and the Xuanzhou District Court "ruled on September 26, 2019 to vacate the effective civil judgment involved in the case"; the article indicates this case-within-a-case comes from the "Typical Cases of Supervision over Civil False Litigation Published by Anhui Provincial People's Procuratorate." |

Grading: A. Three statutory laws + one departmental normative document were verified word for word, and the case comes from official typical cases released by provincial procuratorial organs (reported by Legal Daily, reprinted on the procuratorate's official website). Benefit magnitude: "Large" evaluated mechanically under freedom thresholds (avoiding death penalty and fixed-term imprisonment of 10+ years), costs are zero, placing both rules in the Extremely High cost-effectiveness tier.

Two Deliberate Trade-offs: (1) **Did not cite the Phuket Island, Thailand wife-murder insurance fraud case**. Investigation revealed that case was sentenced to life imprisonment by Phuket Provincial Court in Thailand on 2019-12-24, not by a Chinese court; citing it to explain concurrent punishment under Chinese criminal law was off-topic, so it was swapped for this domestic Xuancheng case. (2) **Main text investigative details are strictly confined to published facts from the case text** (no braking marks, straight turf tracks, policy timing and counts, prior search history, checking beneficiary payout ratios the day after reporting claim), with no speculative extrapolation—the core thesis of this rule is "legally, not a single penny will be obtained," and the investigative breakthrough merely illustrates that "thinking one can get away with it" is an illusion.

Incidental Cross-Reference Fixes: Draft remarks for new Section 9, Rule 21 originally pointed to Section 5, Rule 2 for auto insurance claims and Section 5, Rule 12 for health insurance fraud. In reality, auto insurance is in Section 5, Rule 26, and family mutual aid is in Rule 13; these have been corrected.
