Rating A: Cochrane systematic review presents pooled risk ratios and confidence intervals for three outcomes, numbers verified verbatim with Europe PMC records; free item content comes from current national norms.

The benefit magnitude is "medium" and the caliber is "monetary": the benefit of this is not to reduce mortality - on the contrary, it cites evidence that universal physical examination has no impact on mortality (RR 1.00). What is really exchanged is the money saved on the self-pay package. According to the amount threshold, it falls in the "hundreds to thousands" range, so it is selected; the three costs are all zero, and the cost-effectiveness is combined into "high". The reason why the caliber is not set as the mortality rate is because the income of this item based on the mortality rate will be mechanically represented as "small" (no relative decrease), which will incorrectly express the value of this item.

## Two decisions on writing

**Titles bring their own way out. ** There is a verification record of `Troubleshooting - Only Prohibitions and No Ways Out.md` in the warehouse. This article is written according to that conclusion: the title is "Don't rely on the universal physical examination package to prevent diseases, do several evidence-based screenings according to age and gender." The second half of the sentence is the way out; the notes list the numbers of the nine items in Section 1, so that readers can directly spell out their own lists. Just writing "Don't buy the package" without giving a substitute will be read as "physical examinations are useless", which is a dangerous misunderstanding.

**Free channels are written separately in the income column. ** Some of the realistic answers to "how often should you have a physical examination" do not require you to pay out of pocket: free blood pressure testing every year for those over 35 years old, and free physical examination once a year for those over 65 years old. Writing this part clearly is equivalent to giving specific alternative actions after "not buying the package", and also answers the frequency in the original question.

**Clearly outline situations where this does not apply. ** Notes indicate that three types of medical examinations are not within the scope of this article: physical examinations paid for by work organizations or others; people with chronic diseases or symptoms; examinations prescribed by doctors based on their condition - that is diagnosis and treatment, not screening. Most of the trials included in Cochrane are in high-income countries with good baseline medical accessibility. The remarks indicate that areas with poor medical resource accessibility should not be copied directly.

**Canonical version. ** The "National Basic Public Health Service Standards" cited is the third edition of 2017, which is the current version; the project itself is still being implemented (as evidenced by the interpretation of the State Council's "Notice on Implementing Basic Public Health Services in 2025"). This notice is not quoted this time because the original text of the two service contents comes from the third edition of the specifications.
