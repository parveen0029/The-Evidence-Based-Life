# Section 1 added: Do not pick up or dismantle metal parts of unknown origin (2026-09-19)

Source of the task: The user pointed out that the book lacked the section "things that glow and heat, don't touch unfamiliar metals, it may be a nuclear leak", and cited "dismantling X-ray machines and selling scraps, dismantling radioactive things" and "there are many cases on the Internet", and asked whether the probability of ordinary people encountering it is high.

Original coverage: None in the entire book. grep "Radioactive Source/Radiation/Radioactivity/Nuclear Leak" only hit three irrelevant contents - Section 6 PET-CT article talks about checking one's own ionizing radiation, Section 8 talks about Criminal Law Article 114 when the word "radioactivity" appears in the list of crimes, and Section 28 talks about the prohibition of online sales of radioactive drugs. There is no mention of out-of-control radioactive sources, radioactive sources in scrap metal, or dismantled source-containing equipment.

Point of placement: A new article is added to Section 1, numbered as Article 34 and placed at the end of the section. The number of any existing article is not changed, and cross-section references are not changed. Put it at the end instead of inserting it at the front according to "zero cost, big profit". The reason is shown in the sorting explanation below.

Rating A: Figures all come from official gazettes, ministerial notifications, administrative regulations and official IAEA publications, and can be verified verbatim.

The magnitude of the benefit is "large": the mortality rate should be based on the relative reduction threshold, but there is no relative reduction available (there is no queue for "group that picks up radioactive sources vs. group that doesn't pick up radioactive sources"). The endpoints are determined according to the health endpoint in the "Income" column of the entry - the endpoints are death (4 people in Goiania) and IV degree acute radiation skin damage, which fall into the most severe category, so they are set as "large". All three costs are zero, and the price/performance ratio is considered "extremely high".

The caliber is set on mortality rather than freedom: the subject of this article is health endpoints such as acute radiation injury and death. It does not constitute a crime if the picker himself does not know it. The criminal aspect is not the main line, so the second paragraph of Article 125 of the Criminal Law is not cited and no remarks are included.

## Contents and reasons not included in the text

**Nanjing "5·7" Iridium-192 loss accident (2014) is not cited. ** The case is most closely related to the situation of "passers-by picking up" - Tianjin Hongdi Company lost a Class II iridium-192 source during flaw detection operations in the Sinopec Fifth Construction Company. Two workers saw "something that looked like a necklace" and picked it up and then dropped it on the way to work. The source was out of control for nearly 40 hours. On May 10, it was recovered in the wetland grass about 2 kilometers away. The Nuclear Industry General Hospital treated the affected persons. However, this time no accident report or investigation report that could be verified verbatim was found on the official websites of the Ministry of Ecology and Environment, the National Nuclear Safety Administration, and the China Atomic Energy Agency. The retrieved details were scattered in the work dynamics of local ecological environment departments and reports on CPPCC figures, and were paraphrased. The official original text of "The dose of exposure to the picker was approximately 1.3 Gy" that appeared in the search results was not found, so the entire case was not included in the text. The Goiânia case itself was a case of non-professionals sourcing from discarded equipment, and with complete IAEA data, sufficient to assume the same role.

** "Cesium salts emit blue light in dark places" is not written. ** This is the source of "radiation and heat" in user questions and is widely circulated. However, this expression is not found verbatim in the IAEA materials verified this time. The text takes advantage of this in turn: it clearly states that "most radioactive sources are just ordinary pieces of metal that do not emit light or generate heat" and block the misunderstanding of "judging by the senses". This is more useful than reciting the details of the luminescence, and does not require the unverified description.

**12369 Do not write. ** When drafting, it was originally intended to be "Call 110 and then dial 12369". After verification, it was found that the "12369 Environmental Protection Reporting Hotline" short number has been completely stopped in use in 2024, and is now used as a national ecological environment complaint and reporting management platform. The main text only says to call 110, and "report to the ecological environment department, public security department, and health department" in accordance with Article 42 of the regulations, and does not include a deactivated number.

**The treatment of "Is there any radiation after the X-ray machine is disassembled?" ** User examples are mixed with a common misunderstanding. Article 68 of the Ordinance defines "ray devices" as "X-ray machines, accelerators, neutron generators and devices containing radioactive sources" - under the same category, there are X-ray machines that produce rays when powered on and no residue when powered off, as well as devices that contain sealed sources. Therefore, the note does not generally say "medical equipment is dangerous" or "X-ray machines are fine", but clearly states the demarcation: the danger lies in whether there is a sealed radioactive source in the equipment (radiotherapy machine, gamma flaw detector, well logging instrument, material level meter containing source), and gives enforceable rules - ordinary people cannot tell from the appearance, so equipment parts of unknown origin will not be dismantled or sold.

## Sorting instructions

Section 1 is generally ranked in terms of cost performance. However, although all three costs are zero and the benefits are "large", the basic incidence rate is extremely low: according to the bulletin, the annual accident rate is less than 1 per 10,000 pieces. With about 178,000 sources in use, the number of radiation accidents nationwide every year is in the order of a dozen, and most of them occur in occupational scenarios such as flaw detection, medical, and industrial measurement, and do not fall on ordinary people. Inserting it at the beginning of the section will crowd out the position of high-frequency items such as transportation, gas, and fire, which is contrary to the original intention of "sorting by cost performance" - the expected revenue must be multiplied by the occurrence rate. Therefore, it is compiled in Article 34 at the end of the section. The main text "Speaking People" truthfully states that "the probability of ordinary people being hit is extremely low" without using the density of cases to create panic; the reason for establishing the entry is that the cost is zero and the consequences are irreversible, not that the probability is high.
