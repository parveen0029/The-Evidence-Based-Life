# Added: Should you take action if something happens to a stranger? Verify the record (2026-09-09)

Source of the task: Readers asked "should you save someone when you encounter a stranger calling for help on the road? Should you save someone if you are drowning, falling to the ground and convulsing, a group of people fighting, or should you help an old man who falls to the ground?"

Original coverage: Two of the five questions have already been written - Drowning in section 13 (original section 25, now section 26), and falling to the ground due to convulsions in original section 14 (now section 15), both of which include live action. The vacuum is the level of "do you want to take action?": Searching for "acting bravely when facing justice" only appears once in Section 7, Article 3 (scope of legal aid). Searching for "One Hundred and Eighty-Four" and "Good Guy" yields zero hits (there is a mention of Article 184 of the Civil Code in the remarks of Section 13, Clause 1, which has no independent landing point), and "helping" and "what to do after a fall" yield zero hits - Section 1, Article 13 only talks about how to prevent yourself from falling. Caught in a fight with others, the same zero hit: Section 8, Item 9 is about being involved in a conflict yourself, which does not cover bystanders. Who to turn to for money after rescuing an injured person is not included in the book.

Falling point: 3 new articles added to Section 13. The new Article 2 (What to do if someone falls to the ground or an old man falls) is inserted after the cardiac arrest clause, and the original Articles 2 to 36 are extended to 3 to 37; the new Article 38 (Coming into a fight) is inserted after "being asked for money by a stranger" and before HIV is blocked, the original Article 37 (HIV) is extended to Article 39; the new Article 40 (money for righteous acts) is placed at the end. Section 13 is included instead of Section 8 because these three sections are about what to do first in the tens of seconds at the scene. The legal provisions here are the basis for "dare you do it or not" and are not an independent subject.
