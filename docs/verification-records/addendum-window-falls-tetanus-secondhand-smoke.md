## III. Concerning the Rule "Pushing Through to Your Desk Counts as a Work Injury"

This rule was added in response to reader queries, rooted in popular claims circulated on short-video platforms: an employee feeling unwell insisted on driving to the office before collapsing, and family members received work injury compensation; another employee felt unwell at work, went home, suffered a fatal event, and received no employer payout.

Compared against the statutory text, this popular claim gets causation half-backwards. Article 15 requires a sudden onset of illness "during work hours and at the work post" — the decisive legal factor is **where the individual was when illness struck**, not where they died. If an employee falls acutely ill at their desk and is transported to the hospital, succumbing within 48 hours of emergency resuscitation, the clause may still apply; conversely, for acute medical emergencies occurring on the commute, Article 14 Item (6) covers only traffic accidents, not endogenous medical crises. Therefore, "insisting on driving to the office" is legally far from a guaranteed win, while medically representing an unequivocally hazardous gamble.

Consequently, this rule was drafted as an error-correction rule, calibrated to the mortality metric rather than money: the behavior it aims to change is "call emergency services (120) first rather than rushing to work," with benefit magnitude rated "Large" based on survival probability.
