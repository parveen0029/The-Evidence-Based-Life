# Addendum: What to Do After Being Cyberbullied · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task Origin: After breaking down the sixth tier (secondary dissemination and cyberbullying) in [docs/should-you-stop-for-strangers-in-an-emergency.md](../should-you-stop-for-strangers-in-an-emergency.md), the author inquired whether to add a dedicated rule on "what to do when encountering cyberbullying."

Original Coverage: Previously, only the second half of Section 8 Rule 15 mentioned "preserve evidence first and then report to the police when cyberbullied." The core focus of that rule was "do not become a party to fabricating rumors and insults yourself"; actionable steps after being cyberbullied were limited to half a sentence. A search for "cyberbullying" across the entire book matched only this single rule. Terms such as "injunction against personality right infringement," "doxxing" (human flesh search), and "expedited evidence collection" returned zero hits. The "Provisions on the Governance of Cyberviolence Information" previously appeared only in the long-form article cited above and was absent from the main text in book/. The conclusion was that this was a genuine gap, requiring an additional rule.

Placement: Added as Rule 35 at the end of Section 8, rather than inserted after Rule 14. There are two reasons: First, the tail end of Section 8 (Rules 30 to 34) already forms a cluster on "what to do when your rights are infringed" — extortion/blackmail, false accusations, presumption of innocence, state compensation, and seeking civil damages as a victim. Placing cyberbullying right after fits naturally into the same category. Second, inserting it after Rule 14 would shift 20 subsequent rules, requiring updates to 8 external citations and 11 internal references ("Rule N of this section"), whereas appending it to the end requires zero renumbering. A note pointing to Rule 35 was added to Rule 14.

Network service providers shall provide necessary technical support and assistance to public security organs for evidence collection in accordance with law. Where the conditions for accepting a private prosecution case are met following assistance by the public security organs, the people's court shall decide to file the case; where relevant evidence materials cannot be collected, the public security organs shall explain the situation in writing to the people's court"; Article 12 lists five circumstances that should be recognized as 'seriously endangering social order' under Article 246, Paragraph 2 of the Criminal Law (causing mental breakdown, suicide, or other serious consequences with egregious social impact; indiscriminately targeting members of the general public and triggering substantial vulgar and malicious comments; insulting or defaming multiple persons or repeatedly distributing materials; organizing or directing personnel to extensively distribute materials across multiple platforms; other circumstances); Article 15: "Where the right holder has evidence proving that the actor is committing or is about to commit an illegal act infringing upon their personality rights, and failure to promptly stop it will cause irreparable harm to their legitimate rights and interests, and applies to the people's court for measures ordering the actor to cease relevant acts in accordance with Article 997 of the Civil Code, the people's court may issue an injunction against personality right infringement in accordance with law based on the specific circumstances of the case" |

Rating A: All four are currently effective official documents, and the legal texts themselves provide clear actionable steps and thresholds (which five scenarios convert to public prosecution, mandatory case filing following assisted evidence collection). Benefit magnitude "Moderate" — free calibration: this rule provides readers with stopping infringement and holding perpetrators accountable, on par with Rule 31 (demanding accountability when falsely accused), not marked as "Large". Cost: Money=0, Time=Moderate, Perseverance=Some, synthesized as "Moderate" cost-performance.

In accordance with this round's rule "When stating that the law supports you, you must state the process cost", the notes clearly specify: Platforms are the fastest and cheapest, courts are the slowest and most expensive, so secure evidence and report first; injunctions require court application, private prosecutions require filing and proving evidence on your own, and converting to public prosecution requires meeting one of the five conditions in Article 12, otherwise you can only proceed via private prosecution. Two additional things not to do: Do not engage in flame wars (which turns you into the party penalized under Rule 14), and do not delete content from your own account (that is your evidence).

Omitted / Not cited in the main text: Quantitative evidence linking cyberbullying to suicide (available meta-analyses primarily focus on adolescents, with mismatched demographic cohorts and contexts), providing only helpline 12356 in the notes as an outlet (Section 1 Rule 25). The Cyberspace Administration of China hotline 12377 was not verified word-for-word against an official webpage in this round, so the text only states "prominent expedited reporting portals on platforms" rather than quoting the hotline number. Civil Code Article 1195 (notice-and-take-down) text was not retrieved from the SPP reprint page in this round and thus not cited; platform obligations cite the "Provisions on the Governance of Cyberviolence Information" instead.
