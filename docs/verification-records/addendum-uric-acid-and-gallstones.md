## III. Why a Dedicated New Chapter Was Rejected

The issue proposed a chapter on "Modern Clinical Management of Common Medical Conditions." This was rejected for three reasons:

1. **Genre incompatibility.** Every rule in this book must answer "what is spent and what is gained" as a decision guide for lay individuals. Clinical treatment manuals answer "how to diagnose, which drug to select, and what dosage to administer" — decisions lay readers cannot make and should not execute on their own; treating them as such makes mechanical benefit tiering impossible while introducing substantial direct clinical risk.
2. **The "three highs" are already comprehensively covered; a new chapter would cause extensive duplication.** Blood pressure measurement and treat-to-target medication are in Section 1 Rule 7; fasting blood glucose screening for overweight adults ≥35 is in Section 1 Rule 8; adherence to antihypertensives and statins is in Section 2 Rule 8 (with Level A effect sizes); low-sodium salt, sugar-sweetened beverages, BMI, and exercise span multiple rules in Section 2; chronic disease adherence, 12-week long-term prescriptions, cross-provincial chronic disease reimbursement, and diabetic complication screening constitute the entirety of Section 16; tiered diagnosis and treatment is covered in Section 24.
3. **The genuine gaps were solely uric acid, kidney stones, and gallstones** — a repo-wide search revealed these three conditions were previously mentioned only in passing within remarks of other rules. This round filled precisely these three missing gaps.
