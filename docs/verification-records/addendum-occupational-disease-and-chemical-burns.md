# Addendum: Occupational Disease Protection, Chemical Burns, and Blue-Light Blocking · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task Origin: A reader raised three questions: "Is there treatment for chemical burns, such as acid splashes?"; "Some domestic factories have zero safety measures, causing many accidents and dust exposure with lifelong sequelae lasting decades"; "Is there damage from staring at screens? It's all intense light, and after working 3 months my vision became basically blurred."

Original Coverage: Chemical burns only had half a rule—Section 13, Rule 12 (added in previous round) noted flushing skin and eyes for 15 minutes, but the entire rule was framed around household cleaning agents and accidental ingestion, lacking workplace context, contaminated clothing removal, Safety Data Sheets (SDS), and employer liabilities; Section 13, Rule 6 only covered thermal scalds. Occupational diseases were a complete blank: searching "occupational disease" yielded hits only in the Work-Related Injury Regulations citation in Section 19 and one TODO in Section 23; "pneumoconiosis," "dust," "noise," and "protective eyewear" had zero hits. Screens and eyes were also a complete blank: "asthenopia / eye strain," "dry eye," "blue light," and "myopia" had zero hits.

**Correcting the reader's third claim in the main text**: Everyday computer screens do not cause someone to "lose clear vision after 3 months." What screens cause is digital eye strain and dry eye syndrome, both reversible; intense radiation that inflicts irreversible eye damage over short intervals consists of welding arcs, UV radiation, and industrial lasers—occupational hazards requiring protective goggles, not blue-light blocking lenses. Thus this rule was framed as "Which light genuinely damages eyes and which does not," placed in the anti-checklist.

Placement: Added 1 rule to Section 13 (Rule 13; shifting original Rules 13 to 28 to 14 to 29; cross-reference in docs/home-emergency-kit-checklist.md updated from Rule 16 to 17); added 2 rules to Section 19 (Rules 10 and 11, inserted before work injury block; shifting original Rules 10 to 14 to 12 to 16; section header summary changed to "The final seven rules cover occupational diseases and work injuries"); added 1 rule to Section 6 anti-checklist (Rule 16).

## Section 13, Rule 13 (Chemical Burns)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://niohp.chinacdc.cn/kpdw/202507/t20250722_308659.htm> | Yes | Skin contact: "Immediately flush contact site with copious water for at least 15 minutes"; eye contact: "Immediately flush eyes with copious water or normal saline for at least 15 minutes... pull open eyelids to ensure thorough contact with eyeball and inner eyelids, do not rub eyes. Immediately seek ophthalmological care after flushing" |
| <http://yjt.hunan.gov.cn/yjt/tszt/aqzs/201509/t20150919_3113778.html> | Yes | "For poisons absorbed through skin or corrosives causing chemical burns, immediately remove contaminated clothing, flush with copious water or lukewarm water; hot water prohibited"; another section: "Flush wound with copious running water for 20 to 30 minutes (longer for strong chemicals)"; "Do not apply ointments or mercurochrome to fresh wounds, do not wrap in dirty cloth" |

Graded B: The two official materials give flushing durations of 15 minutes vs. 20–30 minutes without a single unified national standard. Main text takes the longer duration and notes the variance in remarks.

Three deliberate wording choices: (1) "Do not grab another acid or base to neutralize" — Hunan text refers to pre-prepared antidotes/neutralizing solutions at workplace stations, not on-site improvised neutralization; (2) Lime/cement dust: brush off dry before rinsing — local defense mobilization claims regarding concentrated sulfuric acid not rinsing with water immediately lack authoritative consensus and were omitted; (3) Legal linkage to chemical skin/eye burns in occupational disease catalog and Article 37 employer treatment liability.
