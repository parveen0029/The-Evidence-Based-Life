# Addendum: Treating Institutions as People Working by Rules (Four Rules) · Verification Record (2026-09-18)

Task Origin: A cognitive bias raised by a user: "Many people hold a childish mindset... I used to think police exist solely to catch villains and doctors exist purely to save lives, whereas in reality they are working professionals earning a living, bound by duties... Some assume calling the police guarantees immediate processing, unaware of triage prioritization... Others think bribing an officer avoids penalties, forgetting cameras are everywhere, they have colleagues, and there are codified rules." The user was unsure how to integrate this; after presenting three structural options, we selected: "One guiding rule + three operational rules, preserving existing directory hierarchy."

Original Coverage: Searching the book for terms like "call police," "filing a criminal case," "bribery," "red envelopes / kickbacks," and "clean professional practice"—post-reporting statutory workflows and deadlines, legal remedies against refusal to docket cases, police complaint mechanisms, bribery crimes, and hospital anti-kickback red envelopes all had zero coverage; deposit insurance was already covered in Section 5.

Implementation:
- Section 3 Rule 20 (Guiding rule; dimension: time; Grade C): Shift mental model to "professionals working strictly according to institutional rules," directing effort into statutory paperwork and procedural deadlines.
- Section 8 Rule 38 (Post-police-report procedures and remedies; dimension: freedom; Grade A).
- Section 8 Rule 39 (Paying bribes to settle issues = crime of bribery; dimension: freedom; Grade A).
- Section 24 Rule 12 (Expressing physician gratitude via thank-you letters, silk banners, and satisfaction surveys rather than cash red envelopes; dimension: money; Grade A).
Section 3 intro guide added a sentence explaining that the final rule cites regulations rather than empirical trials, rated Grade C, avoiding tension with the section's standard that "figures derive from experimental or cohort studies."

## Trade-offs

- **User's remark about "foreigners and big corporations getting prioritized" was omitted**: That is rumor without official backing; writing it would elevate hearsay to fact. The text instead articulates: "Lawful prioritization is always codified in plain sight" (citing explicit examples like emergency severity triage), adding channels for complaints when arbitrary discrimination occurs.
- **110 Police Dispatch Regulations were not cited**: Internal ministerial normative document absent from State Council public policy database; mps.gov.cn returns 521; thus claims regarding internal emergency dispatch grading tiers were avoided, retaining only verbatim verifiable statutory deadlines and documents.
- **Criminal case filing has no universal "decision within N days" statutory deadline**: Regulations specify "conduct prompt examination"; claims of 7, 30, or 60-day limits lacked statutory grounding in operative regulations. The text strictly limits time limits to administrative security offenses (30 days + 30 days) and post-refusal reconsideration/review deadlines.
- **Rule on bribery omitted "can given bribe money be recovered"**: Dependent on Criminal Law Article 64 and nuanced judicial interpretations not verified article-by-article in this round.
- **Section 24 Rule 12 was revised after feedback**: An initial draft only stated "no need to give cash envelopes" alongside prohibitions, which the user rightly criticized as detached and cold: "My father gave cigarettes to the doctor who saved my life after an all-night emergency resuscitation... are we supposed to give nothing and say nothing?" The critique was valid: the initial text conflated bribing beforehand for priority with heartfelt post-hoc gratitude, prohibiting actions without offering constructive outlets. The revised structure separates these two concepts, offering three concrete ways to express gratitude that tangibly benefit doctors, while explaining that doctors refusing cash envelopes are protecting their medical licenses, not complaining about the sum.
- **Silk banners and thank-you letters entering personnel records is framed as experiential practice**: No formal ministerial document mandates file archiving; the verifiable statutory link is that patient satisfaction survey scores directly determine public hospital performance appraisals.
- **Did not cite the 2014 circular regarding "physician-patient agreements against giving and receiving red envelopes"**: Unretrieved by title in State Council policy database; current validity could not be verified verbatim, choosing omission over uncertain citation.
- **Guiding rule graded C**: Cognitive models lack direct trial literature; cited procedural regulations only substantiate the second half that "deadlines and remedies are statutory," as noted in remarks.
- The three operational rules cross-reference each other, while the guiding rule points to all three; Section 8 Rule 39 and Section 24 Rule 12 illustrate that "giving money to law enforcement" versus "giving cash to doctors" operate under two fundamentally different legal regimes.
