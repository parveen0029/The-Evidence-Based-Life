# Addendum: Legal Options and Red Lines When Unable to Support a Child · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task Origin: A reader saw news of "a postpartum mother strangling a newborn baby and throwing it into a trash bin" and asked whether a relevant rule should be added.

Assessment: The specific criminal act reported in the news does not itself require a rule—no one changes their mind because they read "strangling an infant constitutes intentional homicide." What holds genuine value are the crossroads surrounding it: **where the lawful exits lie when unable to raise a child, and where the boundary between "giving away for adoption" and "selling" is drawn**. This entire domain had zero coverage across the book: searching "giving up for adoption," "adoption," "abandonment," "abduction and trafficking," and "abandoned infant" yielded zero hits across book/.

First checked existing coverage to avoid duplication: Postpartum depression screening is in Section 27, Rule 16; "having thoughts of harming oneself or the child" as a red-line emergency signal is already in the "Go to the hospital immediately" list in Section 27, Rule 7; Section 1, Rule 25 covers handling suicidal ideation; Section 8, Rule 14 covers the right of close relatives to take an individual for psychiatric diagnosis. Thus mental health crises were already addressed; what was missing was the lawful legal outlet.

Placement: Added Rule 19 at the end of Section 9 (appended to the end, zero renumbering). Section 9 is positioned as "legal red lines everyday people easily cross, each rule paired where possible with official cases"; this is precisely a line crossed out of ignorance, carrying severe penalties. Added a cross-reference pointing to it in the remarks of Section 27, Rule 16.

| <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/977.html> (Supreme People's Court portal, Fa Fa [2010] No. 7, March 15, 2010, Joint Opinions of SPC, SPP, MPS, and MOJ on Punishing Crimes of Abducting and Trafficking in Women and Children According to Law) | Yes | Article 17: Distinguishing between selling one's biological child under the guise of adoption and private adoption among citizens, "the key to distinction lies in whether the actor possesses the purpose of making illegal profits"; three circumstances that may be determined as selling a biological child and prosecuted as child trafficking: "Using childbirth as a means of illegal profit, selling the child immediately after birth"; "Knowing that the other party does not have the purpose of raising the child, or completely disregarding whether the other party intends to raise the child, giving away the child in exchange for money"; "Giving away the child in exchange for large sums of money that clearly do not constitute 'nutrition fees' or 'gratitude tokens'"; where individuals, lacking illegal profit motives and forced by living hardships, privately arrange adoption and accept small nutrition or gratitude fees, it constitutes private civil adoption |
