## Rule 3 (Posterior Circulation Stroke: Vertigo, Diplopia, Dysmetria)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169> (PMID 28082668) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | "Of 736 eligible patients with acute ischemic stroke, 14.1% presented without FAST symptoms. When gait imbalance, leg weakness, and visual symptoms were included, the proportion of missed strokes decreased to 4.4%." Authors' conclusion explicitly states "suggested prospective validation before updating public education programs" |

Rated B rather than A: Retrospective single-center analysis over one year (University of Kentucky Stroke Center, 2014); 736 cases provided specific numbers, but this is a single study whose authors themselves called for prospective validation. Benefit magnitude "Large" — evaluated on the mortality metric, where missed diagnosis equates to missing the thrombolysis/thrombectomy time window. Cost tags are all zero; cost-performance tier is Extremely High.

The reader's mention of "cannot touch nose with finger" refers to the finger-to-nose test evaluating cerebellar ataxia, matching the "Balance" component of the BE-FAST mnemonic. The text treats it as a warning sign, not a tool to rule out stroke: no single bedside physical maneuver possesses adequate negative predictive value, so the actionable recommendation is framed as "call emergency services (120) immediately if these symptoms appear abruptly and do not resolve."

## Rule 5 (Aortic Dissection)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Hagan PG, Nienaber CA, Isselbacher EM, et al. (2000). The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD): new insights into an old disease. JAMA, 283(7), 897-903. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.7.897> (PMID 10685714) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | 464 cases across 12 centers; "While sudden onset of severe sharp pain was the single most common presenting complaint, the clinical presentation was diverse." Overall in-hospital mortality 27.4%; Type A medically treated without surgery had a mortality of 58% |

Rated B: Multicenter registry rather than prospective cohort or RCT; sample size of 464 is moderate; mortality figures are observational within the registry. Benefit magnitude "Large" — misdiagnosing dissection as myocardial infarction and administering anticoagulation or thrombolysis is fatal; the value of recognition lies in altering clinical triage. Textbook signs like "pain migrating along the aortic pathway" and "blood pressure differential between arms" lack verbatim extractable proportions in the abstract, so they are documented in remarks as qualitative clinical reminders without unsupported figures.

Omitted figure: The widely circulated statistic that "untreated Type A dissection carries a 1%–2% hourly mortality" originates from Hirst's 1958 autopsy series; primary text could not be verified verbatim in this round, so it was excluded from the main text.

## Rule 6 (Thunderclap Headache and Subarachnoid Hemorrhage)

| Reference | Verified | Source Figures / Excerpts |
|---|---|---|
| Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248-1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018> (PMID 24065011) | Yes (Europe PMC abstract verbatim) | Prospective multicenter cohort of 2,131 patients with "headache peaking within one hour", of whom 132 (6.2%) had subarachnoid hemorrhage; Ottawa SAH Rule achieved sensitivity of "100% (95% CI, 97.2%-100.0%)", specificity of "15.3% (95% CI, 13.8%-16.9%)"; rule criteria include age 40 years or older, neck pain or stiffness, witnessed loss of consciousness, onset during exertion, thunderclap headache, limited neck flexion on examination |

Rated A: Prospective cohort across 10 Canadian emergency departments; sample size and confidence intervals complete. Benefit magnitude "Large", cost tagged as "Money=Few, Time=Moderate" (emergency head CT and waiting half a day for results); cost-performance tier is High.

The text emphasizes "speed to peak intensity" rather than "severity of pain," because enrollment in the rule required reaching peak pain within one hour. The 15.3% specificity is highlighted in the remarks to prevent readers from assuming that meeting a criterion indicates a high probability of hemorrhage.

## Rule 20 (Heatstroke Rehydration)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| China CDC "Guidelines for Public Prevention and Emergency Treatment of High-Temperature Heatstroke (2014 Edition)" <https://niohp.chinacdc.cn/sndt/201405/t20140530_97623.htm> | Yes | "Regardless of activity level, fluid intake must be increased; do not wait until thirsty to drink water" "Drink at least 2 to 4 cups of cool water per hour (500–1000 ml); water temperature should not be excessively high; drink small amounts frequently" "Do not consume alcoholic or highly sugary beverages. These beverages cause further loss of body fluids. Additionally, avoid drinking excessively cold iced drinks"; heatstroke protocol states "do not give water to the patient"; cooling methods include "soaking the patient in a bathtub of cool water; placing the patient under a cool shower; wiping the patient's body with cool water; applying cool wet towels or ice packs to head, armpits, and groin" |
| US CDC/NIOSH. Heat-Related Illnesses. <https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html> | Yes | Heat stroke: "Cool the worker quickly... With a cold water or ice bath, if possible"; Heat exhaustion: "Encourage frequent sips of cool water"; Heat cramps: "Drink water and have a snack or a drink that replaces carbohydrates and electrolytes (such as sports drinks) every 15 to 20 minutes" "Avoid salt tablets" |

Rated B: Both represent official institutional clinical protocols, lacking quantitative effect sizes on "how much mortality drops when followed." Benefit magnitude "Moderate" — rehydration itself is supportive care; the life-saving interventions are physical cooling and emergency transport.

**The reader's claim was both accommodated and delimited in the text**: China CDC guidelines indeed state "avoid excessively cold iced drinks," so "do not immediately gulp ice drinks during heatstroke" has official provenance and was included in the main text; however, the guideline's stated rationale is fear of gastric spasms, lacking quantitative empirical evidence, whereas the same guideline explicitly recommends cold water immersion, cool showers, and ice packs as first-line cooling methods. The remarks clarify this distinction: cold is for external bodily application, not for internal ingestion — avoid misinterpreting "do not drink ice water" as "do not use cold cooling." The second half addresses salt: drinking purely plain water after profuse sweating dilutes serum sodium; both sources recommend electrolyte-containing beverages over plain boiled water.
