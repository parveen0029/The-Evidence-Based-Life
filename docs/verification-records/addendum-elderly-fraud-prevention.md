# Addendum: Senior Care Investment and Reverse Mortgage Scams · Verification Record (2026-09-08)

Task Origin: Addressing the second outright void identified during the audit round sparked by "requests to add anti-scam material." Section 17 previously touched this topic only in Rule 3 (segregating seniors' funds into an independent account, requiring dual-confirmation for large expenditures) and Rule 4 (conversational shield scripts); both were Grade C with zero citations, merely stating "harvesting seniors in a single blow after building long-term emotional trust," without identifying specific fraudulent schemes or statutory legal consequences. In the earlier "Addendum: Chronic Care, Long-Term Care Insurance, and Personal Data Rights," it was noted: "Official cases on elder fraud (reverse mortgages, healthcare product sales presentations): citable SPP typical guiding cases unretrieved, omitting this rule in Section 17"—retrieved successfully in this round.

Implementation: Section 17 added 2 new rules (Rules 5 and 6); original Rule 5 (long-term care insurance) shifted to Rule 7. Rules 3 and 4 were retained intact without modifying grades or sources. No other section cites Section 17 by rule number, requiring no cascading updates.

Splitting into two rules rather than one: The first category involves paying money in exchange for future commitments (VIP cards, apartment beds, senior living communities, migratory retirement, healthcare products), where the preventive action is "never hand over money," with financial loss bounded by the senior's liquid cash. The second category involves mortgaging the senior's home to borrow cash and reinvesting it into fake wealth management products, where the preventive action is "never mortgage, never sign," risking loss of the home while remaining saddled with debt. The financial magnitudes and legal remediation paths differ fundamentally.

## Section 17 Rule 5 (Illegal Fundraising in Senior Care Investments)

| <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202306/content_6886556.htm> (Ministry of Civil Affairs Risk Alert, republished on gov.cn, 2023-06-15) | Yes | Five manifestations: (1) "Illegal fundraising under the guise of elderly care institutions" (no physical facility, using rented temporary premises or claiming partnership agreements to associate with legitimate institutions); (2) "Illegal fundraising under the pretext of providing 'elderly care services'" (promising services far exceeding bed supply, selling "VIP cards," "membership cards," "prepaid cards," collecting advance service fees); (3) "Illegal fundraising under the guise of investing in 'senior care projects'" (investing in wellness/senior living bases, selling fictional retirement apartments, long-term leasing of care beds, "time bank" mutual care projects, promising high yields, buybacks, or leaseback returns); (4) "Illegal fundraising via sales of 'senior products'" (merchandise buybacks, consignment sales, consumer rebates, free health checkups, gift giveaways, conference marketing seminars, health lectures, free clinics); (5) "Illegal fundraising under the banner of 'migratory senior living'" (low-cost or free tours, stored-value rebates, investment dividends, points-based care). Three fundamental risks: promised high yields fail to materialize, fund principal security is unbacked, and care service needs remain unmet. |

## Section 17 Rule 6 (Reverse Mortgages / Housing-for-Pension Scams)

| URL | Verified | Source Excerpt |
|---|---|---|
| <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html> (Hunan Local Financial Supervision Bureau reprint, source credited: CBIRC, 2021-06-08) | Yes | CBIRC Consumer Protection Bureau 2021 Risk Alert No. 3. Legitimate products: "Legitimate housing-for-pension refers to reverse mortgage insurance for seniors, an innovative commercial pension insurance combining home mortgages with life annuities. Seniors owning full legal property rights mortgage real estate to insurance companies, retaining possession, use, usufruct, and disposition rights (with insurer consent), receiving annuities per terms until death"; "Currently in pilot stages and relatively niche in China"; Scams: "The 'housing-for-pension' touted by criminals is completely unrelated to the state's pilot reverse mortgage insurance, serving merely as a promotional pretext for illegal fundraising... often operating as Ponzi schemes paying old investors with new funds. Some victims do not even realize their property was mortgaged, ultimately losing their homes while saddled with debt"; Risk thresholds: "If wealth products promise yields above 6%, question them; above 8%, they are dangerous; above 10%, prepare to lose all principal; 'guaranteed principal with high yield' is financial fraud"; Caution: "Treat contract signing with extreme care; never sign blank contracts." |

Graded B: Derived from regulatory risk warnings and empirical rule-of-thumb thresholds; the 6%/8%/10% figures are consumer evaluation heuristics rather than statutory baselines, lacking quantitative trial metrics on victimization rate reduction. Benefit magnitude rated "large": what is forfeited is real estate property, coupled with outstanding loan debt.
