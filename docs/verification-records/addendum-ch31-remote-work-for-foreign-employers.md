# Addendum: Working Remotely for Foreign Companies from Home · Verification Record (2026-09-20)

Task Origin: A reader asked: "Are there key legal and regulatory precautions for working remotely in China for overseas employers?", and mentioned a rumor they heard that employees of foreign employers must hold that country's citizenship.

Original Coverage: Searching the book for terms like "remote work," "work from home," "digital nomad," "foreign employer," "overseas company," "cross-border employment," and "foreign-related labor" yielded zero rules covering the scenario of "residing physically in China while working remotely for an offshore entity." The five closest existing touchpoints addressed entirely different scenarios: Section 31 Rule 14 (working abroad checks for foreign labor cooperation operational licenses) governs physical departure abroad; Section 32 Rules 3 and 4 (international student work hours and legal status) presuppose a local overseas study visa; Section 11 Rule 5 (refusing freelance development for gambling and scam apps) governs developer red lines; Section 9 Rules 5 and 6 address moonlighting traps; Section 5 Rule 2 (individual income tax annual reconciliation) covers only domestic comprehensive income, without mentioning foreign income anywhere in the section. Implementation point: Added 1 new rule to Section 31 (Rule 15); the original Rule 15 (entrepreneurial guaranteed loans) shifted to Rule 16.

Placement Rationale: Inserted after Rule 14 (working abroad), the two rules together form the outbound and non-outbound sides of "working for foreign employers," allowing cohesive reading. Checked across the entire book: no other section references Section 31 by specific rule number; the only intra-section rule reference is "see Rule 3" in Rule 3's own remarks, which precedes the insertion point. Therefore, this insertion causes zero cross-reference breakage.

## Grading and Trade-offs

- Graded A: All four are verbatim verifiable regulatory provisions forming an unbroken chain of determination: physical service location in China (Implementation Regulations Article 3, Item 1) -> domestic source income -> paying party has no establishment in China, withholding cannot be executed -> triggers statutory self-declaration requirement under Individual Income Tax Law Article 10, Item 2 -> FX remittance settled under current account non-commercial settlement, routed through Article 10, Item 7 "employee remuneration".
- **Dimension and Magnitude**: Dimension: money. Benefit magnitude judged as "medium", not mechanically derived from thresholds — this rule secures avoidance of tax under-reporting and prevents FX remittance blockages rather than netting a fixed lump-sum monetary payout; since monetary thresholds do not apply directly, professional judgment assigns "medium", recorded here. Costs: Money = 0, Time = medium (annual reconciliation settlement once a year, plus preparing bank paperwork), Willpower = some, placing cost-effectiveness in the "average" tier.
- **Most Counterintuitive Aspect**: Laypeople assume "working for a foreign enterprise = offshore income." In statutory reality, source is determined by the physical location where services are rendered, making it domestic income. Neither the foreign tax credit framework (Ministry of Finance & SAT Announcement [2020] No. 3) nor the tax exemption for non-domiciled individuals' foreign income (Implementation Regulations Article 4) applies. The main text translates this distinction into plain language.
- **Four Deliberately Omitted Topics** (all carry potential real-world costs, but verbatim verifiable primary authority was unavailable this round, choosing omission over speculation): (1) Whether this income requires VAT filing or issuing surrogate tax invoices; (2) Whether long-term freelance practice requires business licenses or individual proprietorship registration; (3) Whether the worker can assert labor rights against the offshore employer under Chinese labor contract law (paid annual leave, overtime, severance compensation); (4) Cross-border data transfer compliance and NDAs. These four are explicitly listed in the remarks so readers know they remain uncovered.

## Reader Claim Regarding "Foreign Employers Requiring Employees to Hold Local Citizenship"

**Omitted / Not cited: Not a single word of this assertion appears in the main text.** (Post-merge correction: Previously, the remarks noted "the widespread online rumor that ... was not verified by official sources," which improperly quoted an unsubstantiated reader claim before critiquing it—violating the rule against quoting rumors. This was deleted). Research findings: Under US export controls, the concept of a `U.S. person` (citizen, lawful permanent resident, protected refugee/asylee) does cause certain export-controlled technical positions to exclude non-US persons in practice. However, this is the legal effect of the "deemed export" rule under export controls, not a labor law requirement mandating employer hiring quotas. Furthermore, that applies to disclosing controlled technical data to foreign nationals employed inside the United States, which does not correspond to someone residing in China performing remote work. **Crucially, all retrieved sources were law firm commentary articles (Covington, Pillsbury, etc.) rather than official text of ITAR/EAR or BIS/DDTC authoritative guidance.** Under project citation rules, secondary law firm commentary cannot be cited, so it was excluded from the text. If included in the future, verbatim citations must first be retrieved from eCFR for ITAR (22 CFR 120.62) and EAR (15 CFR 772.1).

## Post-Merge Correction (2026-09-20): Classifying Tax Category Strictly as "Labor Remuneration" Was Incorrect

The original rule's plain-language summary stated "pay domestic personal income tax as labor remuneration," and the benefits column only cited the labor remuneration definition under Implementation Regulations Article 6, Item (2). This step failed scrutiny:

- Implementation Regulations Article 6, Item (1) specifies that "wages and salary income refers to wages, salaries, bonuses obtained by individuals by virtue of holding office or employment..." (Verified verbatim against gov.cn <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/22/content_5351177.htm>). The rule is titled "Working for Foreign Employers," and the text instructs readers to bring an employment contract to banks for FX settlement under "employee remuneration," yet told them to declare taxes under labor remuneration—an internal contradiction.
- The rule's remarks admitted that "what legal relationship exists between a foreign employer and a remote worker lacks a verifiable statutory source." If the underlying legal relationship is legally undefined, the tax category cannot be dogmatically asserted.
- This is not semantic: in financial reality, Article 6 of the Individual Income Tax Law allows a statutory 20% expense deduction for labor remuneration, author's remuneration, and royalties before inclusion into gross taxable income, but wages and salaries do not receive this percentage deduction (verified in State Taxation Administration policy library <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5193028/content.html>, 2018 seventh revision Article 6). The computed tax differs significantly between the two categories.

Correction: The plain-language text was amended to "consolidate into annual comprehensive income and self-declare domestically," differentiating: "signing an employment contract under the employer's operational direction = wages and salaries; taking on independent project deliverables = labor remuneration," noting that labor remuneration includes only 80% of revenue into taxable income. The benefits column added Implementation Regulations Article 6, Item (1) and Individual Income Tax Law Article 6 source citations; the sources column added Article 6 and a link to the SAT policy database. In remarks, item (3) of omitted topics was narrowed to "whether labor rights can be asserted against offshore employers under labor law," decoupled from tax classification; repository-internal workflow terms were excised. The core deductive chain (service performed in China → domestic income → no withholding agent → self-declaration → FX remittance under employee remuneration) remains intact.
