# Addendum: Systematic Audit of Overstepping Statements in Immunity and "What Is Penalized Is Not X" Clauses · Verification Record (2026-09-09)

Task Origin: Section 13, Rule 2 was criticized for anchoring the fear of "being extorted / framed" entirely on Article 184 of the Civil Code, whereas that article only immunizes against "harm caused to the aided party in the course of rescue," failing to cover "the victim turning around and falsely asserting that you hit them." The author requested a comprehensive audit of the entire book for identical vulnerabilities.

Audit Criteria (Two Rules): (1) **Beneficiary Misalignment**—The rule's default scenario involves a stranger, yet costs and benefits are drafted as if "it is your duty to manage it"; (2) **Statutory Overstepping**—Using a statutory provision that only governs Immunity A or narrows Scope A to pacify Risk B.

## Audit Scope

- Beneficiary Misalignment: Scanned all 471 rule titles for keywords (stranger, bystander, passerby, counterparty, colleague, friend, neighbor, Good Samaritan, rescue, etc.), hitting 37 rules; fully resolved in the same day's four-tier beneficiary audit round with no new issues found.
- Statutory Overstepping: Scanned the entire book for phrases like "not bear liability," "not liable," "no compensation needed," "immune," "not illegal," "does not constitute," "not pursued," "not a crime," as well as "as long as... it is not considered," "will not be filed as a case," "no need to worry," and "what is penalized is not...", reading original provisions rule-by-rule to compare statutory scopes. Found 2 genuine issues and 2 cases confirmed clear upon verification.

## Modification 1: Section 9, Rule 3 (Consuming Overseas Content Without Forwarding)

Original remarks stated: "The law penalizes 'incitement,' 'dissemination,' and 'provision,' not 'viewing.'" While true for Articles 103 and 105 of the Criminal Law cited in that rule, it was phrased as a universal conclusion, overlooking an express statutory exception:

Added Criminal Law Amendment IX to sources.

## Modification 2: Section 8, Rule 15 (Online Speech and Cyberbullying)

Original remarks stated: "Crimes of insult and defamation are in principle 'handled only upon complaint,' meaning one must privately sue in court; police will not proactively file a criminal case." The general direction was correct, but "will not" was overly absolute, missing two statutory exceptions within the article itself:

|---|---|---|
| <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> (Criminal Law Article 246, 1997 text includes paragraph 2) | Yes | "Except where serious harm is caused to social order and national interests." |
| <https://www.gjxfj.gov.cn/gjxfj/fgwj/flfg/webinfo/2017/03/1601761496614423.htm> (Criminal Law Amendment IX newly added Article 246 Paragraph 3) | Yes | "Where acts stipulated in paragraph 1 are committed via information networks and the victim complains to a people's court but encounters genuine difficulty providing evidence, the people's court may request assistance from public security organs." |

Revision: Changed "police will not proactively file a case" to "police generally do not proactively file a case," and added both exceptions to Remarks. Added Criminal Law Amendment IX to sources.

## Two Instances Confirmed Free of Issues

- Section 8, Rule 29 (Strict Liability for Dog Keeping): Correctly distinguished Civil Code Article 1245 (intentional act or gross negligence of victim can mitigate/waive liability) from Article 1246 (violating management rules without safety precautions: mitigated only if victim acted intentionally); both Plain Language and Benefit clearly state that "failing to leash eliminates the defense of gross negligence," avoiding overstepping.
- Section 8, Rule 15 penalty numbers (detention days, fines, prison terms) align with the 2025 revision of the Public Security Administration Punishments Law and Criminal Law Article 246; only "will not proactively file a case" needed adjustment.

## Round 2: Missing Procedural Costs (Added Same Day)

The author pointed out that citing Article 90 of the Civil Procedure Law Interpretation in Section 13, Rule 2 to soothe "fear of extortion" remained inadequate: when surveillance fails to identify the hit-and-run vehicle, the reporting bystander who left their phone number is the easiest target for family members to pursue; phone harassment, door-to-door visits, and being named as a co-defendant can all occur. Article 90 only guarantees the final verdict, not avoiding entanglement; winning the lawsuit still costs months of time and out-of-pocket legal fees. This represents the third pattern of statutory overstepping: **using substantive outcome rules to pacify procedural process risks**.

Article 29: "Litigation costs shall be borne by the losing party, except where the prevailing party voluntarily bears them..." |

Attorney fees are not among the three items enumerated in Article 6; thus "borne by the losing party" under Article 29 does not cover attorney fees—the main text states "even if you win, you still pay your own lawyer fees," derived directly from statutory enumeration.

Revisions: Section 13, Rule 2 cost column added "If targeted by family members, defending the lawsuit takes months, and legal fees are excluded from 'borne by the losing party'"; remarks changed "essentially drops to zero" to "surveillance, companions, and dashcams reduce the risk of losing, but cannot eliminate the cost of getting dragged in; these two ledgers must be calculated separately," noting statutory adjudication time limits and court fee scopes. CLAUDE.md added a project rule: "When writing 'the law supports you' rules, procedural costs must be stated."

Scanned the entire book for phrases like "can sue / can claim / can recover / win lawsuit / entitled to compensation" to ensure process costs are stated:

- Added process costs to two rules (modifying only the Cost column without citing new figures): Section 5, Rule 9 (child in-app purchases refund: "if talks break down and you sue, it takes months, attorney fees self-funded") and Section 14, Rule 8 (personal information deletion rights: "suing takes months, attorney fees self-funded; filing an administrative complaint is far more economical").
- Four rules already correctly accounted for process costs and serve as standard templates: Section 8, Rule 21 (online shopping scams: provides full formula of after-tax hourly wage * estimated time + court fees * probability discount of post-judgment enforcement); Section 8, Rule 32 (false accusations: notes "do not treat pursuit as a foregone conclusion" and explains burden of proof gaps); Section 5, Rule 22 (prepayments: notes "this rule provides ex-post legal recourse, not ex-ante peace of mind—if the merchant runs, the money is gone"); Section 8, Rule 34 (state compensation: notes compensation-obligated organs, upward application procedures, and exclusion of attorney fees from state compensation).

## Round 3: Workplace Harassment and Job Loss (Added Same Day)

The author added another cost layer: relatives might cause disturbances at the reader's workplace, prompting the employer to fire them. Verified in the 2025 revision of Public Security Administration Punishments Law, reprinted by the SPP <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>:

- Article 26: "Whoever commits any of the following acts shall be sentenced to a warning or a fine of not more than 500 yuan; if the circumstances are relatively serious, to detention of not less than 5 days but not more than 10 days, and may concurrently be fined not more than 1,000 yuan: (1) Disturbing the order of organs, organizations, enterprises, or public institutions, rendering work, production, business, medical treatment, teaching, or scientific research unable to proceed normally, without causing serious losses..."; "Whoever gathers a crowd to commit acts in the preceding paragraph shall, for the ringleaders, be sentenced to detention of not less than 10 days but not more than 15 days, and may concurrently be fined not more than 2,000 yuan."
- Article 50 Item (5): "Repeatedly sending obscene, insulting, or threatening messages, or adopting harassment, pestering, stalking, or other methods to interfere with another person's normal life" shall be punished by detention of not more than 5 days or a fine of not more than 1,000 yuan; under relatively serious circumstances, detention of 5 to 10 days and a fine of up to 1,000 yuan; "Whoever commits harassment, pestering, or stalking in Item (5)... may, upon approval by the head of the public security organ, be ordered to be prohibited from contacting the victim for a prescribed period."

Article number cross-verification: Itemizations under Article 50 (Item 2 public insult/defamation, Item 3 fabricating facts to frame others) in the same scrape match citations previously verified in Section 8, Rules 15 and 31; thus Article 26 was accepted with confidence.

Framing followed this round's rules: Do not stop at "the employer cannot fire you like this." Remarks explain that termination on these grounds generally constitutes unlawful dismissal subject to 2N severance (cross-referencing Section 19, Rule 6), but filing police reports and labor arbitrations require substantial personal effort; initial job loss cannot be undone by future 2N severance. Thus the conclusion remains "walking away is lawful and saves the most trouble," not "rely on legal backstops."

The portrait right provision was the most counter-intuitive discovery of this round, and the only "you may not be able to stop it" note in the long-form article: fair use in news reporting means on-site filming does not necessarily require your consent; all you can control is whether to accept interviews, show your face, or speak.
